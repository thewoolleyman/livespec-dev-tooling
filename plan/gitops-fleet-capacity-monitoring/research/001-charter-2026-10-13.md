# gitops-fleet-capacity-monitoring — charter

Opened 2026-10-13 at the maintainer's direction, by the hp storage session (livespec-dev-tooling-smrzsj, closed).

## What this plan is

A **long-lived standing session** for the GitOps-managed fleet hosts (hp-xubuntu, poweredge-xubuntu, gmktec-xubuntu, vps). The maintainer uses it to ask ad-hoc questions about the fleet and to handle capacity and host incidents. Each concrete piece of work it takes on is a child work item. Questions answered in passing need none.

**Expiry.** This plan retires when the factory hosts move onto the Kubernetes infrastructure and these bespoke GitOps machines are retired, as the maintainer stated on 2026-10-13. Its Definition of Done is therefore deliberately narrow: the one capacity decision in flight at creation. Standing work beyond that is admitted as child work items and recorded in scope events.

## Where the fleet's capacity knobs live (this repo)

- hp's Fabro fleet cap: `ansible/inventory/host_vars/hp-xubuntu.yml`, `fabro_server_instances` → fleet instance `max_concurrent_runs: 10`. It has been live since 2026-10-13T07:11:52Z.
- Applying a cap change takes a Fabro server restart, which ends in-flight runs. The path is an inventory edit, then `just ansible-apply ansible/fabro-hosts.yml --limit hp-xubuntu`, with the other roles skipped and `-e fabro_server_manage_settings=true -e fabro_server_restart_allowed=true -e fabro_server_interrupt_runs_allowed=true`. The maintainer has ruled: **no restarts and no killing of in-flight runs without asking.**
- The sandbox envelope: role `docker_sandbox_slice` (`fabro-sandbox.slice`, MemoryMax 24G, CPUQuota 1400%), shared by every Docker container on hp.
- Swap and container storage: role `sandbox_storage` (160 GiB swap on sdc1, stores on sdc2).
- Method for setting any cap: `.ai/ci-node-capacity-reads.md`. The cap is throughput-derived (successes per hour), not utilisation-derived. hp's 15-minute-load target of 24 (150% of 16 cores) is a SOFT cap, and going over it is not by itself a reason to step down (maintainer ruling 2026-10-13). The decisive signals are OOM kills, successes per hour and the infrastructure-type failure share.

## Driver session rules (maintainer rulings 2026-10-14, scoped to this plan)

- **Never present a blocking prompt to the maintainer.** No `AskUserQuestion`, no picker,
  no "shall I". A blocked driver has no watcher, so every tab it coordinates loses its
  coordinator at once. Decide every operational question yourself (including whether an
  owner may remove a live run), record the decision as a scope event or ledger comment,
  and report it. A genuine spec or values call goes to the maintainer as a non-blocking
  line in the running report. Ruled 09:09Z after the driver raised a picker.
- **Coordinator, not executor.** The driver does not dispatch, reconcile, or implement.
  It wakes owner tabs by `herdr agent prompt`, tells them the specific next step, and
  opens a Claude Opus tab named after the plan slug for any plan that has none. Ruled
  08:40Z after the driver dispatched and reconciled work itself under handoff 5's wording.
- **Read the scope events before the handoffs.** Handoff 5 drifted from the 00:05Z ruling
  and the next session executed the drift. When a handoff and a scope event disagree, the
  scope event is the maintainer's direction and wins.
- **Audit the queue before you write about it (maintainer ruling 2026-10-16 ~15:5xZ).**
  A resume or tick entry written while two or more hp slots are free MUST carry the
  output of the idle-slot audit (`research/002-idle-slot-audit-2026-10-16.md`), run over fresh `bd list --status all --json`
  dumps of all three tenants (dev-tooling, orchestrator, livespec) and the live board.
  The audit classifies every non-closed child of every priority epic as dispatchable,
  stale edge (blocker merged and released but not closed), needs capture or accept,
  needs approval, unadmitted, or host-only, and names the owner. Idle slots with
  dispatchable work is a FINDING reported to the maintainer with idle slot-hours,
  never a quiet tick. A handoff's "nothing can use the slots" is a pointer to
  re-measure, not a measurement: on 2026-10-16 that sentence was repeated across three
  driver restarts while nine slots sat idle for five hours over four dispatchable items
  (a stale blocks edge, an over-narrow reading of the priority set, unadmitted
  priority-plan backlog, and uncollected host captures). Rulings that hold work carry a
  reconsider time and are re-read each tick; every owner states its binding constraint
  at session start and in each scored report, and "host-bound" is a prompt to ask what
  would make it faster, not a terminal classification.
- **The coordinator resolves blocked decision pickers (R301, maintainer direction
  2026-10-20 ~11:4xZ, confirmed by the maintainer 2026-10-20 ~21:5xZ).** When a seat
  pane is blocked on a decision picker, the coordinator resolves it itself: it decides
  under the decision-authority rules, answers in that pane, and messages the seat that
  it decides its further steps itself. It never parks a pane as a maintainer item. Only
  genuinely maintainer-owned calls are surfaced, each as one exact question:
  credentials, irreversible or outward-facing acts, and workflow-edit approval labels.
  R301 covers DECISION pickers only. The coordinator never answers a TOOL-PERMISSION
  prompt in another seat's pane; those go to the maintainer. This retires R199 (no
  keystrokes into panes) for decision pickers; the ledger RULES entry on
  livespec-dev-tooling-dfixhe stays the authoritative record.
